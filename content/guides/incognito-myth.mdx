# Incognito isn't privacy

Open an incognito window and the little mascot winks at you as if it's your accomplice. It's not.
"Private/incognito" mode does exactly one thing well — and people routinely overestimate it.

> **Image: An annotated screenshot of a browser's incognito new-tab page, with two columns:
> "What it actually does" and "What people think it does", each with 2-3 short bullets.** — placeholder.

## What incognito actually does

- Stops your browser from **saving history, cookies, and form data on your device** during the
  session.
- Clears most of that when you close the window.

That's the whole job. It's for sharing a computer, not for hiding from the internet.

## What it does NOT do

- **Hide you from websites.** They still see your IP address, your browser fingerprint, and
  everything you type into forms. You're not anonymous to the site — you're just a stranger to your
  own history.
- **Hide you from your ISP, your employer, or the network.** The traffic still flows through them
  in plain sight from their side.
- **Stop tracking.** Cross-site trackers and fingerprinting still work in incognito.

## When it IS useful

- Logging into a second account without a cookie clash.
- Checking your own site as a fresh visitor.
- Using a borrowed or public computer.

## What to use instead, for real privacy

- **Tor Browser** for serious anonymity — it layers your traffic across volunteers' relays.
- A **privacy-focused browser + VPN/DNS** for everyday leak-reduction.
- A **private search engine** so your searches aren't archived into a profile.

## The honest takeaway

Incognito is a convenience, not a security tool. Forcing yourself to "think it's private" is exactly
the kind of calm, confident assumption the snail spends its whole life distrusting.

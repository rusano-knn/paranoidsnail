# How to pick a VPN without a headache

Walk into any VPN vendor's site and the headline is some version of "Double-encrypted military-grade
anonymous invisible shield!" It's marketing, not information. Let's do this honestly.

> travel), columns = "what a VPN does" vs "what it doesn't", plus a verdict column.** — placeholder.

## First: what a VPN actually does

- **Hides your traffic from your ISP**: it encrypts between your device and the VPN server, so your
  ISP sees "encrypted traffic to a server," not the sites you visit.
- **Changes your apparent IP address** to the VPN server's.

## What it does NOT do

- **Make you anonymous.** The VPN provider still sees your real IP and what you're sending
  (unless it runs a strict no-log network, and even then, trust is required).
- **Protect you from malware, phishing, or your own bad decisions.**
- **Fix a browser that leaks.** Fingerprinting, cookies, and login accounts still track you through a
  VPN.

## The non-negotiable checklist

1. **Independent, verifiable logging policy.** "We keep no logs" means little on its own; look for
   published privacy policy and, ideally, a **third-party audit**.
2. **A real "no account needed" or anonymous-account option** (like an account number instead of an
   email) — a strong signal of a privacy-first provider.
3. **Open-source clients** where possible, so the software's claims are inspectable.
4. **No surprise "free tier" that turns your traffic into a business** — if you're not paying, you
   need to know how they make money.
5. **Reputable ownership** — check who's actually behind it and where it's incorporated, because
   jurisdiction shapes what they can be compelled to reveal.

## Red flags to walk away from

- Ads promising "total anonymity" or "military-grade" with no receipts.
- Free services that are really ad networks or sell your data.
- Vague logging claims, no audits, no company info.

## The calm verdict

A VPN is one useful layer in a much bigger picture, not a magic cloak. Buy the boring, transparent,
audited one that matches the three honest questions from [threat modeling](/guides/threat-modeling),
and don't expect a hoodie and a green screen to follow you around.

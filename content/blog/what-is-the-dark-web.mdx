# What the dark web actually is (and isn't)

Most people's mental model of the "dark web" was installed by a movie trailer: glowing green text,
a figure in a hoodie, and a countdown to something ominous. The reality is far less dramatic and, in
some ways, far more interesting.

> a calm, non-alarmist tone.** — placeholder.

## Three nested layers, not one scary place

- **Surface web:** what search engines index — public pages anyone can reach. That's most of what
  you browse daily.
- **Deep web:** everything **not** indexed by search engines — your email inbox, your bank's
  dashboard, private databases, paywalled journals. It's the vast majority of the internet and it is
  completely mundane.
- **Dark web:** the small slice of the deep web that sits behind special software (like **Tor**) and
  uses non-standard addresses (like `.onion`). This is what people usually *mean* by "dark web,"
  and it's a tool, not a place with a mood.

## Why it's not inherently evil

The dark web's defining feature is **anonymity and censorship resistance**. That makes it a refuge
for journalists, whistle-blowers, and people under oppressive regimes — and, yes, a place where
criminals and scammers also hide. The technology is morally neutral. It's what people choose to do
with it that isn't.

Treat broad panic about "the dark web" like broad panic about "the internet" — a category error.

## The honest risks

- Scams, phishing, and malware are **common** there, precisely because anonymity lowers the cost of
  bad behaviour.
- A lot of content is plainly illegal and carries real legal risk in most jurisdictions.
- "Clearinghouses" of links are often unvetted.

None of that makes the dark web radioactive. It makes it a place where **calm, verification, and
good judgment** matter more than anywhere else. Which, if you ask the snail, is a fine skill to
practice anywhere.

## Bottom line

The dark web is a tool for reaching unindexed, encrypted services — some good, some bad, most
forgettable. It's not a place to "visit for a thrill." It's a place to use deliberately, with your
head on straight, the same way you'd use any powerful tool.
